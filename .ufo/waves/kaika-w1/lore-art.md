### Current art and fit

1. **Series bibles are format-sensitive references, not one proven universal schema.** The Writers Guild Foundation (WGF) teaches differences among episodic, serialized, sitcom, and other formats; its archive/library documents actual scripts, pitches, notes, treatments, and show bibles. A WGF exhibit describes a *The Wire* bible artifact with a character-name variant, a useful reminder that a bible is a working reference, not infallible canon. The WGA MBA's terms are contractual categories and compensation rules, not a universal world-bible template. **[INFERENCE]** For Lore, build a comparative checklist from public exemplars and retain only fields useful to the lane's fictional records; do not import television format assumptions wholesale.
2. **Wikis and graph tools offer different layers.** World Anvil documents article templates/links and visual faction relationships. Dataview's primary docs describe indexed note metadata and query/display views, explicitly not editing. Wikibase models assertions as statements with qualifiers and references; SHACL validates RDF graph structure against shapes. None of these features, by itself, establishes that a fictional source supports a claim or that a claim is canon. **Fit:** compare data portability, scoped claims, source attachment, relationships, temporal/event queries, validation, and human review before selecting a tool; keep Lore's existing JSON/checker evidence intact.
3. **A much richer continuity-error taxonomy is now available.** The 2026 ConStory-Bench paper proposes five broad classes and 19 subtypes: timeline/plot logic; characterization; world-building/setting; factual/detail consistency; narrative/style. It includes duration, simultaneity, causality, abandoned plot elements, character memory/knowledge, skill/ability, social norms, geography, appearance, names, quantities, point of view, tone, and style. This is a taxonomy for long LLM-generated stories, not a production-industry standard; use it as a comparison/candidate test list, not as automatic acceptance criteria.
4. **Plot-hole and temporal benchmarks separate useful failure modes.** The 2025 FlawedFictions work constructs plot holes in human-written stories and tests detection; its task is model reasoning, not validating a lore database. Qiu et al. (NAACL 2024) test event duration, chronology, and temporal self-consistency, including reversed before/after questions. StoRM (Findings 2022) represents a reader's beliefs about a fictional story world as a graph, a distinct view from an authorial canon graph. These are complementary frames, not equivalent notions of truth.
5. **A 2025 editable-graph storytelling study is promising but bounded.** Pan et al. evaluate a KG-assisted generation workflow with 15 participants; the abstract reports gains concentrated in action-oriented, structurally explicit narratives, not introspective stories. It justifies exploring editable graph representations, not claiming that a graph checker can certify story quality or canon. LongStoryEval (ACL 2025) evaluates book-length stories through reader-review aspects; it is useful for broader evaluation but is not a continuity-error benchmark.
