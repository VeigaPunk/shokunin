# Craft apprenticeship and expertise pedagogy in the shokunin lifecycle

**Scope:** Fleet-level research probe; read-only. Local protocol evidence reviewed: `PROTOCOL.md` §§Lifecycle/Promotion gates, `LEARNING.md` §§Learning path/Discernment/Province, and `AXES.md` §§Working the axes/Capabilities. External sources are listed only when opened in this session. This is a literature-to-protocol mapping, not an evaluation of a shokunin lane or evidence that the protocol itself causes expertise.

## Findings at a glance

The shokunin lifecycle already has a strong evidence-backed shape: shonen begins with scoped work; shokunin must demonstrate end-to-end work and leave executable instructions; jin'i-ryu must propose and land an improvement to its own skill/province from telemetry. The recurring learning path is curation → quality gating → understanding → frontier exploration. Non-author spot-checks, explicit reasons for rejection, falsifiable before/after claims, and capability demonstrations provide independent feedback rather than relying on confidence or time served.

The expertise literature supports those mechanics, with two guardrails. First, treat shu-ha-ri and Dreyfus as interpretive models, not universal rank taxonomies. Second, practice volume and experience are not themselves evidence of mastery: the useful unit is a task linked to feedback and an observed change, and intuition is credible only where cues and outcomes are sufficiently learnable. Transporting findings from martial arts, music, workplaces, or clinical decision-making to model-based craft is an **analogy**, not a demonstrated causal result.

## Mapping the research onto the lifecycle

| Framework | What it predicts about expertise growth | Protocol mechanics supported or challenged | Concrete protocol refinement implied |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shu-ha-ri** (守破離) | A traditional-arts learning heuristic: first reproduce a form under guidance (shu), then test/understand and adapt it (ha), then act independently or make a style (ri). The current sources describe the relationship among learner, instructor, form, and mastery; they do not validate a universal timetable. In a small judo-kata teaching study, student-generated personal expression preceded formal expression in the task studied. | Supports shonen’s guided formation and jin'i-ryu’s independent iteration as a useful analogy. Challenges interpreting shonen → shokunin → jin'i-ryu as three mandatory, one-way pedagogical stages, or as a requirement to finish imitation before any bounded exploration. | State in the protocol that shu-ha-ri is an analogy, **not** the promotion rubric. Add a lesson field recording the cycle actually used—model/form, guided attempt, variation or counterexample, and what principle survived—without requiring the same order in every domain. Promotion remains artifact- and verifier-based. |
| **Deliberate practice** (Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer, 1993) | Improvement is associated with effortful, structured tasks designed to improve a current performance aspect; the task should fit the learner, provide informative feedback/knowledge of results, and be repeated or refined. Their account distinguishes deliberate practice from work, play, or mere exposure. | Supports falsifiable round claims, before/after measurements, executable capability demonstrations, and rechecks. Challenges treating corpus growth, time-on-task, repeated execution, or a high score by itself as evidence of improved craft. | Require each performance-improvement claim to name its *practice unit*: target behavior, baseline, designed task, feedback received, next attempt, and observed result. Retain aggregate hours/counts only as context, never as the result. |
| **Deliberate-practice debate and critiques** (Macnamara et al., 2014; Ericsson and Macnamara et al., 2016; Macnamara & Maitra, 2019) | Practice is useful but does not account for most between-person performance differences in the meta-analysis: pooled estimate was about 12% of variance, with substantial domain variation (e.g. 26% games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, under 1% professions). Ericsson’s critique says measures grouped unlike activities and hours; the reply contests that reading and stands by the meta-analysis. The 2019 violin reanalysis did not reproduce the original ordering of all three groups, while still finding a substantial, smaller association. These are correlational and measurement-sensitive results, not proof practice has no causal benefit. | Supports local metrics plus explicit uncertainty, and challenges a universal hour threshold, “practice alone explains skill,” or importing one discipline’s effect size as a fleet-wide target. It also challenges silently assuming that studies measured the same construct. | For each numeric claim, log exactly what the numerator/measure represents, the domain, the performance outcome, and the limitation; separate practice-process measures from outcome measures. Do not use a universal practice-hour total or the popular “10,000-hour rule” as a promotion condition. |
| **Cognitive apprenticeship** (Collins, Brown & Newman; Collins, Brown & Holum) | Expertise grows when normally hidden expert thinking becomes observable: modeling, coaching, scaffolding then fading, articulation, reflection, and exploration. Learning should use meaningful tasks and varied contexts so that learners can recognize transfer rather than memorize a single example. | Supports shonen’s scoped skill/checklist and end-to-end task, self-contained instructions, coaching through lessons, and the move toward independent execution at shokunin. It challenges a gate that sees only a polished artifact but cannot tell whether the learner can explain or transfer the judgment that produced it. | Add one “show the work” item to a round lesson: a short decision trace/model, a coached attempt with feedback, and a later independent or varied-context attempt with reflection. Use it to improve transfer evidence, not to demand private reasoning beyond what can be safely and usefully observed. |
| **Legitimate peripheral participation / communities of practice** (Lave & Wenger, 1991; Wenger, 1998) | Learning is changing participation in a social practice: newcomers gain legitimate access, take on meaningful work, learn tools/discourse, and develop identity while moving toward fuller participation. Access and local organization shape what can be learned; a single designated master-apprentice relationship is not universal. Workplace research cautions against assuming every setting offers one smooth route from periphery to center. | Supports the shonen apprenticeship concept, real domain tasks, growing demonstrated capabilities, and review from outside the author. Challenges reducing growth to a repository’s private corpus or assuming rank alone confers membership, access, or recognition. It cautions against making one mentor the sole authority. | Add practice-context evidence to new capability records: the authentic contribution, the role/access the lane had, and who used or reviewed the result. Keep the named non-author verifier separate from the author; permit evidence from multiple practitioners or audiences. |
| **Dreyfus skill-acquisition model** (Dreyfus, 2004 summary of the Dreyfus brothers’ model) | In a given skill, learners move from context-free rules, through recognition of situational cues and chosen plans, toward experienced perspective and intuitive response. The model also describes increasing responsibility for choices and learning from their outcomes. It is a conceptual account, not a validated fleet-wide rank test. | Supports shonen’s explicit rules and checklists, shokunin’s situational judgment and independent capability demonstrations, and jin'i-ryu’s telemetry-guided self-improvement. It challenges a single scalar expertise score or stage label for an entire lane; different tasks can remain at different levels. Mistakes matter when a learner owns a choice and reflects on its outcome, not as shame or an uncontextualized error count. | In one lesson per round, capture a decision trace for a representative task: rule(s) used, situational cue(s), chosen plan, predicted outcome, actual outcome, and adjustment. Do not assign a Dreyfus stage to the lane or turn mistakes into a quota. |
| **Conditions for expert intuition** (Kahneman & Klein, 2009) | Skilled intuition is pattern recognition acquired where the environment supplies sufficiently valid cues and the learner has adequate opportunity to learn them. Subjective confidence alone does not indicate accuracy; delayed, ambiguous, or absent outcome feedback can build overconfidence instead of skill. Expertise is task-specific/fractionated. | Strongly supports non-author verification, explicit evidence, calibration, and careful treatment of frontier work. Challenges self-attested “we know it works,” intuition-based promotion, or interpreting repeated success in noisy/low-validity work as mastery. | When a claim relies on judgment or intuition, record a prediction/cue and its observable outcome (or explain why no reliable outcome exists). State the feedback delay and validity limit; if outcomes cannot test the judgment, leave the claim unproven or use the existing exploratory/non-author-review path rather than asserting expert accuracy. |

### Lifecycle synthesis

- **Shonen:** Give legitimate, bounded access to a real task. Model the whole task and make its decision points visible; use checklists/rules as scaffolding, not as the permanent definition of expertise. The current shonen → shokunin gate’s independent execution and non-author checks are compatible with this.
- **Shokunin:** Build a practice loop from domain work: choose a weakness or frontier question, perform a deliberate task, get informative feedback, refine, and show independent transfer. Preserve rejection reasons as evidence of discernment, but do not confuse rejected candidates or errors counted with learning.
- **Jin'i-ryu:** Self-iteration means proposing and landing falsifiable improvements from telemetry; it does not mean self-certifying the improvement. Retain outside review for taste-based claims and report the limits of outcome feedback, especially in unstable domains.
- **Across ranks:** The protocol’s curation → quality gate → understanding → frontier loop fits research and practice models better than a fixed examination. It should stay a repeated cycle, not be recast as an irreversible maturity ladder.

## Study paths

1. **From activity to defensible practice claim:** Ericsson et al. (1993) → Macnamara et al. (2014) → Ericsson’s 2016 commentary → Macnamara et al.’s 2016 reply → Macnamara & Maitra (2019). Start with the proposed mechanism, then read its measurement challenge and response, then inspect a preregistered reanalysis. The order preserves disagreement rather than flattening it.
2. **From apprenticeship to autonomous craft:** Lave & Wenger (1991), ch. 1 → ch. 4 → Collins et al. (1991) → Dreyfus (2004) → Kahneman & Klein (2009). Start with participation and access, add visible modeling and fading, then consider situational judgment and test when intuition can be trusted.
3. **From tradition to adaptive form:** Bradić et al. (2023) → Reguli (2018) → Calmet et al. (2024). Read the contemporary concept, a martial-arts teaching interpretation, then the direct classroom study that qualifies a strict sequence.

## Mistakes and rejected simplifications

These are **literature-derived lessons**, not mistakes mined from lane round logs; no lane mistake corpus was in this probe’s scope.

### M01 — Do not turn the “10,000-hour rule” into a promotion threshold

The 1993 paper argues for long, domain-specific preparation and deliberately structured activities; it does not establish one universal number of hours. Later estimates vary by domain and by measurement. A threshold rewards accumulated time, not demonstrated ability to improve or perform.

### M02 — Do not treat Shu-Ha-Ri as a proven universal three-step exam

The direct study is a specific judo-kata teaching case, and its conclusion allows personal expression before formal expression. The opened evidence does not establish an invariant order across martial arts, much less across software, research, or other shokunin domains.

### M03 — Do not infer that practice explains all performance variance

The 2014 meta-analysis reports highly heterogeneous associations and leaves most performance variance unexplained; its outcomes are not causal attribution. The 2019 violin study did not reproduce the original three-group correspondence. This is a reason to triangulate measures and retain uncertainty, not to dismiss practice.

### M04 — Do not treat time, repetition, or error totals as improvement

Ericsson’s practice account requires informative feedback and a designed task; Kahneman and Klein require valid cues and a chance to learn them. A repeated failure with no valid outcome signal may not teach anything, and a high raw count can hide no change.

### M05 — Do not let confidence, rank, or one mentor stand in for demonstrated expertise

Kahneman and Klein explicitly reject subjective confidence as a reliable accuracy signal; Lave and Wenger’s chapter summary notes that a specific master-apprentice relationship is not universal. Use the current non-author evidence gate and authentic participation, not authority or self-description alone.

## Contract defects and limits

No defect was established in the inspected protocol passages: the lifecycle, verifier/evidence requirement, outside spot-checks, rejected-item reasons, exploratory-claim rule, and capability demonstrations are explicit. The refinements above are opportunities to make the learning mechanisms observable, not claims of existing nonconformance. The main unresolved risk is interpretive: readers could mistake the lifecycle names for fixed pedagogical stages, although the operative promotion gates are artifact-based.

**Inference boundary:** All mappings from martial-arts, cognitive-apprenticeship, workplace, music-practice, skill-acquisition, and expert-judgment literature to the shokunin lifecycle are design inferences. No opened source tests shokunin itself, language-model improvement, non-author verification as a causal intervention, or the effectiveness of these proposed fields.
