## Answers for the integrator

1. **No sibling lead implements `checklist-item → runnable command → per-item verdict`.** Kotoba is closest: it maps hash-pinned boxes to dispositions and runs named static/file checks, while its command check only establishes that command heads resolve. Kumitate's proxy counts boxes; its round gate checks round artifacts. Kasegi checks Markdown citation anchors. Kengaku replays experimental data. Kake's located script is a history report, not a checklist gate. Kagi's 16-item command/verdict gate is therefore not duplicating an existing sibling's command-runner scope.
2. **Useful shared shape: “stable-ID outcome ledger.”** Give each item/check a stable ID and explicit disposition/result with a reason or evidence pointer; make the final verdict and exit status unambiguous. Kotoba's pinned item-to-disposition map and the other tools' named check IDs are precedent for that *shape*. Do not reuse `executed` to mean arbitrary command execution, or force their unrelated exit policies and schemas into kagi's TSV.
3. **Relay, don't absorb:** (a) Kasegi `anchorcheck.py`'s detected-bad-but-exit-0 behavior as a separate owner/integrator issue; (b) Kake's state-changing/metered/operator-owned action boundaries as Kake scope, not kagi checklist rows; and (c) any cross-lane doc/citation or evidence ownership finding to its lane/integrator. Keep kagi's gate restricted to its 16 credential-hygiene items and its stated no-vault/no-handler/no-secret-output constraints.
